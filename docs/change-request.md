## 1. Request

> "Many members return a book one or two days late because the library is closed on Sunday or they come after
> work. Charging 0.20 AZN for that annoys them and the staff spend more time at the desk collecting small coins
> than the fine is worth. Please do not charge a fine when a book is returned **at most 2 days** after the due
> date. If it is returned later than that, charge the normal fine for every day late, as today."

## 2. Analysis of the request

| Question | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Is the fine for a 3-day-late return 3 × 0.20 or 1 × 0.20? | **3 × 0.20 = 0.60 AZN** (every day counts once the grace period is exceeded) | The request says "the normal fine for every day late". This is also how Koha's grace period works (Appendix A of the requirements). |
| Does the grace period change when a loan is *overdue*? | **No.** A loan is still overdue from the day after the due date (BR-06) and still blocks new checkouts and renewals. | The request is only about money. Keeping BR-06 unchanged keeps the blocking rules and their tests stable. |
| Does it change the maximum fine? | No, still 10.00 AZN per loan (reached at 50 days late). | Not requested. |
| Does it apply to fines already charged? | No. Charged fines are stored per loan (`loan.fine_qepik`, models §4.3) and are not recalculated. | A charged fine is a historical fact; the stored value makes this automatic. |
| Should the length be configurable? | Yes, as a business rule value `grace_days = 2`. | Same approach as every other rule value (requirements §7). |
